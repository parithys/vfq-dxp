## Journey Overview

Catalogue #89 is the **approval workflow** side of Transfer of Number (TON) — the queue-based review/sign-off steps a Trouble Ticket (TT) passes through before a TON is executed, explicitly modeled as "the same functionality as Adjustment Approval" (row #30: approving based on an **Approval Matrix and Queue**) applied to TON tickets. It sits downstream of the retail/back-office TON *creation* flow (documented separately in Day7 Session 1 and Day4 Session 2) and upstream of the actual number-transfer *execution* (account/service-account/profile change on Siebel/Vanilla, documented at [01:05:56] in source #2).

TON is a **prepaid-only** ticket type in the current Trouble Ticket system (source #4) and was **deliberately built as a fraud control**: source #4 documents a historical fraud case where a fraudster collected payment from two different people for the same number at two different stores within about an hour — this is why TON requires **four sequential approvals routed to the CDM (Customer Data Management) team** rather than being auto-executed [Day4 Session 2, 00:49:37–00:51:44].

The workshop's stated to-be direction is to automate as much of this approval chain as possible via configurable business rules (e.g. SAR/star-number status, contract minimum stay) — targeting **~70-80% automation** (source #3, [01:02:04]) — with the remaining ~20-30% still routed through the manual queue-approval chain modeled here.

## Workflow Steps (numbered)

1. **Ticket enters queue.** A TON ticket is raised (from the retail/back-office TON creation journey — out of scope here) and lands first in the **Managing queue**, which is described as "auto-opened since three years" (i.e. a standing, always-open queue) [source #2, 00:54:53].
2. **Managing queue triage/hand-off.** The ticket sits in the Managing queue as the entry point of the four-queue chain before being routed onward [source #2, 00:54:53].
3. **CDM (Customer Data Management) review.** The CDM team (named individuals Tisha and Krishna referenced as CDM contacts) reviews the ticket "as a guide" and checks whether the request follows the configured business rules; only after CDM approves does the ticket move on to the teams that execute the change (i.e. transfer the number) [source #2, 00:00:09; also 01:05:44 "CDM team (customer data management) — Reviews and checks tickets against business rules before approval"].
4. **CDM decision point** — approve or reject (see Decision Points below). Common rejection reasons captured: missing/incorrect TON form attachments, missing required documents, signature/QID data-entry errors [source #2, 00:26:51, 00:26:09].
5. **Routing fork to back-office queue by nationality.** After CDM approval, the ticket routes to **either BOQ or QATQ depending on the customer's nationality** [source #2, 00:55:14 "The ticket routes to BOQ or QATQ depending on the customer's nationality... a non-star number should not require that approval"; 00:55:26 "five activities are visible: ... and finally the BOQ or QATQ depending on the customer's nationality who validate and close"].
6. **BOQ / QATQ validation and close.** The assigned back-office queue (BOQ or QATQ) performs its own validation pass and, on approval, **closes** the ticket [source #2, 00:55:26].
7. **Back-office execution.** Once all approvals are through, the back-office team selects/assigns the ticket, and on the existing customer's account changes the account name, service account name and account profile to the new customer, updates the order line items, and submits a single TON order (no plan change) under the new customer account [source #2, 01:05:56–01:06:39]. This execution step is technically the fulfillment step referenced by the catalogue description ("...to execute the change, meaning transfer the number" [source #2, 00:00:09]) — included here for completeness though it sits just outside the pure "approval" scope of #89.
8. **SIM swap gated on success.** SIM swap takes effect **only upon TON success**; if rejected, the old SIM remains active with the original customer [source #2, 00:40:47; also 00:37:26 mandatory SIM swap in all cases including eSIM-to-eSIM].

Ticket-activity trail confirmed in source #2 [00:55:26]: the system records five activities per TON ticket — (1) the SMS, (2) the agent who raised the ticket, (3) the agent who approved it manually, (4) the CDM validation, (5) the BOQ or QATQ validation/close.

## Decision Points

1. **CDM review: does the request satisfy business rules?**
   - **Approve** → ticket proceeds to the nationality-based BOQ/QATQ fork (step 5).
   - **Reject** → ticket returns to the requesting agent/customer via SMS notification; common causes are missing TON form/attachments, missing required documents, and signature/QID data-entry errors [source #2, 00:26:51, 00:26:09, 00:01:20 "SMS as the only interaction channel today... reject, follow up and similar"].

2. **Back-office queue (BOQ or QATQ) validation: approve or reject?**
   - **Approve** → ticket closes at the back-office queue and moves to execution/fulfillment (step 7).
   - **Reject** → SMS sent to notify; per the risk log, if rejection happens **after** the number was already disconnected, the number must go through number management and re-research, and the customer can permanently lose it — explicitly flagged as something "that must never happen" [source #2, 00:31:26, 00:53:37].

3. **(Upstream/business-rule fork, not a human decision node but affects routing) Is the number a star/free number with no recorded value?**
   - If the number price is null (free number or star number with no legacy price on record), the current system design incorrectly routes it into the CDM queue as if it required CDM approval — flagged as a **design gap**, not a genuine business requirement; the to-be ask is that a non-star, no-value number should **skip** CDM approval entirely [source #2, 00:56:25, 00:57:26, 01:00:51].

*Note on scope:* The evidence describes a **sequential chain of team/queue reviews** (Managing → CDM → BOQ/QATQ) each of which can pass or reject, rather than named "approve/reject" UI buttons at each stage individually documented; the two decision points above are the ones with explicit reject-path detail in the transcripts. The Managing-queue step is documented as a routing hop, not as an independent approve/reject gate — no source describes a distinct Managing-queue reviewer or rejection action there.

## Exception Handling

- **Missing attachments / document errors at CDM stage** — rejection with SMS notice; agent must fix and resubmit [source #2, 00:26:09, 00:26:51].
- **Rejection after disconnection already happened** — flagged as the most severe failure mode: the number becomes effectively unrecoverable / must go through number management re-research; explicitly called out as a scenario "that must never happen" and a live risk in the current design because disconnection timing is not always gated behind full approval [source #2, 00:31:26, 00:53:37, 00:45:47].
- **SIM already issued to the new customer, then TON rejected** — inconsistent state: the physical/eSIM sits with the new customer while the account is still with the old one; to-be requirement is SIM swap should only take effect on TON success [source #2, 00:39:56, 00:40:47].
- **Power of Attorney (POA) exception path** — when the existing (transferring-out) customer cannot be present, a POA/authorisation letter is accepted instead (used in under ~5% of BAU cases, typically for travel or medical reasons); a standardized POA document format is a stated requirement [source #2 and #3, 00:51:13, 01:08:56 region; Day7 Session1 references POA-based exceptions in the four-queue routing summary].
- **Null/blank number price causing unnecessary CDM routing** — legacy data gap (no historical sale price recorded for some old numbers, not correctable even post-migration); currently forces tickets that shouldn't need CDM approval into the CDM queue anyway [source #2, 00:56:25, 01:01:44, 01:00:20].
- **Third-party TON on a cancelled ID** (one of the three TON types) — detected reactively via an MOI-shared file that BI turns into hold-down and invalid-ID tables; bulk notes are uploaded to Siebel monthly and an SMS is sent to eligible users who must bring the physical SIM/eSIM screenshot plus the SMS to a store — this is an upstream trigger path into the same approval chain, not a distinct approval exception, but shapes which tickets enter the queue [source #3, 00:43:33, 00:44:38].
- **Escalation beyond CDM/BOQ/QATQ** — not documented in any source; **flagged as unknown**. No transcript describes an escalation tier above the four queues (e.g., supervisor override, SLA-breach auto-escalation). Day4 Session2 notes that automatic escalation generally is only an aspirational "wish list" item for the trouble-ticket system as a whole, not something confirmed for TON specifically.

## Business Rules

- **Fraud-control origin:** TON requires four sequential approvals specifically because of a documented historical fraud pattern (same number sold to two different people at two different stores within about an hour) [source #4, 00:49:37–00:51:44].
- **Prepaid-only today:** TON as a Trouble-Ticket type is prepaid-only in the current system [source #4, 00:49:37].
- **Four-queue sequence:** Managing queue → CDM → (BOQ or QATQ, chosen by customer nationality) [source #2, 00:54:53, 00:55:14].
- **Star-number protection:** transfer of a star number (valued ≥1,500 Riyal) requires a customer data restoration/declaration form listing recent usage, with a clause letting Vodafone reclaim the number if the transfer is later found fraudulent; requires either a one-year postpaid commitment scaled to the number's value, or payment of the star number's value if moving to prepaid; the commitment restarts from month one regardless of the prior customer's progress; the penalty is collected manually in cash via WinCash under a separate SKU [source #3, 00:45:59–01:03:51, 00:52:23–00:58:13].
- **Non-star/no-value numbers should skip CDM approval** (to-be requirement; today they incorrectly route into CDM due to the null-price design gap) [source #2, 00:57:26].
- **Segregation of duties:** manual verification must be performed by a person different from the one who entered the data [source #2, 00:28:29].
- **SIM swap gating:** SIM swap only takes effect on TON success, in all cases including eSIM-to-eSIM [source #2, 00:37:26, 00:40:47].
- **Pre-validation requirements (to-be):** age validation (must not be a minor, via MOI/date-of-birth, noting a 5-8 day MOI sync-lag risk); neither party may be in collections — checked **before** entering the TON flow, not inside it; full DOI and any remaining lock-in/park-my-number penalties must be cleared before transfer [source #3, 01:00:35, 01:12:57–01:15:53].
- **Automation target:** ~70-80% of TON cases automated via configurable business rules (SAR number, contract minimum stay), remaining exceptions routed to manual approval tickets [source #3, 01:02:04; source #4, 00:51:09].
- **TON fee:** BAU/mutual TON carries a 50 Riyal fee, collected as cash (not billed), with payment receipt attached; who pays it (customer vs. store discretion) is explicitly an **open/unresolved** question blocking automation [source #2 fee detail corroborated in Day7 Session1, 01:10:53–01:11:25].
- **Three TON types drive different intake paths into the same approval chain:** (1) BAU — both parties present, happy path; (2) COO/claim-of-ownership — only the current user present, on hold ~2 years but retained in-system; (3) third-party TON on a cancelled ID — detected reactively via MOI/BI hold-down tables [source #3, 00:28:37–00:44:38].
- **Catalogue-level rule (row #89):** approval access is granted "based on Responsibilities" (i.e., role/queue-membership gated, mirroring row #30 Adjustment Approval's "Approval Matrix and Queue" model) — no further detail on the specific responsibility/role matrix is given in any source; **flagged as unknown** pending a dedicated approval-matrix document.

## Data Inputs / Outputs

**Inputs to the approval chain:**
- TON ticket (created upstream) carrying: customer A / customer B accounts, attached CAF, TON form, QID/passport/DCC/diplomatic ID documents, free SR for QID update, and (for star numbers) the customer data restoration/declaration form [source #3, 00:45:59–00:47:23].
- Number value/price (from Siebel) — used to decide whether CDM approval and star-number handling apply [source #2, 00:54:53, 01:00:51].
- Customer nationality — used to route to BOQ vs QATQ [source #2, 00:55:14].
- SMS as the sole notification channel for reject/follow-up communication at each stage [source #2, 00:01:20].

**Outputs from the approval chain:**
- Ticket activity trail: SMS record, raising agent, manually-approving agent, CDM validation record, BOQ/QATQ validation-and-close record [source #2, 00:55:26].
- On full approval: an executed TON — account name, service account name, and account profile updated on the existing account/installed assets, order line items updated, single TON order submitted under the new customer account (no plan change) [source #2, 01:05:56–01:06:39].
- On rejection: SMS notice to the relevant party; ticket returned/reworked; old SIM remains active if a SIM had not yet been swapped [source #2, 00:40:47].
